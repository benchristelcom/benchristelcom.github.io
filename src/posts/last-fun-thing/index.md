There was a time when I found myself simultaneously listening to an Alan Watts
lecture on Asian religions, and watching an unnarrated stream of a WWII flight
simulator. Someone was flying a fighter plane towards a dramatic sunset. On a
whim, I fullscreened the second video. And suddenly, I was in the head of that
fighter pilot. Completely focused, utterly alone. Nothing but air for miles
around me in all directions. Nothing but the sun and the roar of the engine in
my face.

In that moment, I found a strange calm. I had no fear of death, but I knew I would
fight hard to survive. I had no hatred for my enemy, but I knew I would bring
all my skill to bear to shoot him down. My body had become a machine — a
machine that I was merely _watching_, as if it were part of the aircraft. 

And at the same time, I heard Alan Watts say, in his wry, unhurried baritone:

> It seems to me that the most interesting question in all of philosopy is:
> _What am I?_

_What am I?_ Am I this body? Am I the plane? Am I my enemy? Am I the sun and
the wind? Or am I beyond all of them? If I am going to make it out of this alive,
what should I do now? And if I am not going to make it out of this alive, what
should I do now?

And I began to understand.

I began to understand that time is not a vessel to be filled. It is only the
page number of a story we are being told. Whether life is long or short, fast
or slow, makes no difference. Wait as long as you like; it will never not be
_now_.
